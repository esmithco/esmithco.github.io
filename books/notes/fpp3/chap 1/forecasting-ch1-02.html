Framing the Forecast

1 · Define→ 2 · Frame→ 3 · Model→ 4 · Quantify
Before you forecast, decide what, decide when, and decide what data you'll need.
Three decisions, in order
  • 1 What exactly — a single product or event, or a whole quantity? one line vs. a product group
  • 2 Time term — days, weeks, or months? And how often it updates — daily or weekly? ↖ set by business or problem necessity
  • 3 Only then — the data itself: what to collect, how, and where to find it. That's what decides the right model and methodology. → the payoff

Types of forecasts

Qualitative

Time series
A time series is events observed sequentially, in order, over time.
The question it answers: how will it behave next?
e.g. quarterly profit · temperature across seasons
observed forecast (median) 80% / 95% interval
sampled at regular or irregular intervals
Limits of a simple time series
  • * Doesn't explain the factors behind the behavior.
  • * Misses seasonal patterns.
  • * Ignores outside information — marketing pushes, other business conditions.
✦ Decide what, when, and what data — the model comes last. A time series just asks: how will it behave next?
