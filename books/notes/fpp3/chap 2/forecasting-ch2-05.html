Time Series Graphics — Plot First

Before you model, look. To understand a problem or the data, the best first move is to plot it — each kind of plot answers a different question the raw numbers hide.
Which plot answers which question
  • time plotwhat's the overall story — trend, seasonality, cycles, anomalies?
  • seasonal plotwhat's the repeating shape within a season?
  • subseries plothow does each season behave and change over time?
  • scatterplothow do two series relate? (correlation)
  • lag plotdoes a value depend on itself k steps ago?
  • ACFhow much memory does the series have — and where?
The three patterns you're hunting
Trend
Long-run rise or fall. No fixed period.
e.g. suscripciones creciendo año a año
Seasonality
Fixed, known period tied to the calendar (week, month, year).
e.g. pico de ventas cada diciembre · cierre de mes
Cycle
Rises & falls of no fixed period, usually longer (> 2 yr), driven by conditions.
e.g. ciclo económico / de negocio
seasonality ≠ cycle
Seasonal — length is fixed and known (period = 12 months, 7 days…). Repeats on the calendar.
Cyclic — length varies and is usually longer; you can't set its clock in advance.
Time plot  ·  §2.2 — the default view
read left → right: trend up, seasonal wobble, a one-off spike (outlier)
  • look fortrend · seasonality · cycles · anomalies (spikes, level shifts) · gaps.
  • compareplot several series together (color = hue) or split by variable, group, or aggregation.
Seasonal & subseries plots  ·  §2.3–2.4
seasonal plot: each year drawn on top of the same axis — the shape that repeats
  • seasonalcollapse time onto one season and overlay every cycle → see the recurring shape and which periods are high/low.
  • subseriesone mini-panel per season (one for Jan, one for Feb…) with its mean line → how each season evolves across years.
Scatter & lag plots  ·  §2.5–2.6 — relationships
  • scattertwo series against each other. Correlation measures linear strength & sign: r ≈ 0.25 weak r ≈ −0.99 strong, inverse.
  • lag plotplot yt vs yt−k. A tight diagonal cloud = strong autocorrelation at lag k (often at the seasonal lag).
watch — correlation only catches straight-line relationships and never proves cause. A curved pattern can have r near 0 yet be perfectly related.
Autocorrelation (ACF)  ·  §2.7 — the memory of the series

ACF = correlation of the series with its own past, at each lag. It's the fingerprint of trend and seasonality:

left: trend (slow decay) + seasonality (spike at lag 12) · right: white noise
  • trendACF stays high and decays slowly — nearby values are alike.
  • seasonalspikes at the seasonal lags (12, 24… for monthly). "does last month matter? does it repeat a year later?"
  • white noise§2.8 — no autocorrelation: every bar inside ±2/√T. Nothing left to model; this is what good residuals look like.
✦ Plot first. Time plot = the story · seasonal / subseries = the repeating shape · lag / ACF = the memory · white noise = nothing left to model.
