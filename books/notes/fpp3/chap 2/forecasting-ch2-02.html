Time in Pandas

Two kinds of time. A Timestamp is a point; a Period is an interval. Everything else is either a collection of these (an Index) or a converter (a function).
Don't confuse Python's datetime module (with its datetime.datetime class) with pandas' own time objects below. They look alike; they are not the same.
The two atoms
●
Timestamp — a point
pd.Timestamp("2020-01-01 08:00") — one specific moment.
[──)
Period — an interval
pd.Period("2020-01", freq="M") — the whole month of Jan 2020.
The two indexes

An Index is the structure of labels that identify rows. Labels need not be numbers, and need not be unique (df.index.is_unique checks). When the labels are time, you get one of these:

  • DatetimeIndexMany timestamps (points). freq="8h" means spaced every 8h — not that each covers 8h.
  • PeriodIndexMany periods (intervals). dtype="period[8h]" means each element is an 8h interval; the label shown is its start.
  • to_datetime()The converter function: one value → Timestamp; a list → DatetimeIndex.
The distinction that matters for forecasting
● DatetimeIndexObservations occur AT these points in time.
[──) PeriodIndexObservations belong TO these time intervals.
Coarsening timestamps into periods can repeat labels — four readings in the same week all become one weekly period. That's not an error; it means they belong to that week. Want one row per week? aggregate them.
