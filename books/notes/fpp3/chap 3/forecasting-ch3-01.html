Decomposition — Splitting the Signal

Sometimes a series is too tangled to model directly. So split it into parts you can read and forecast on their own: a trend-cycle, a season, and what's left.
The two ways to split
yt = Tt + St + Rt
additive — parts add up
yt = Tt × St × Rt
multiplicative — parts scale each other
additive vs multiplicative
Additive — seasonal swing stays the same size as the level rises.
Multiplicative — seasonal swing grows with the level.
Take log(y) and a multiplicative split becomes additive: log y = log T + log S + log R.
What each part really is
Trend-cycle  TtThe smooth long-run movement and the slow ups/downs (cycles) — bundled together. "Where the series is heading once you ignore the wiggles." Estimated by smoothing.
Seasonal  StThe pattern that repeats every fixed, known period (12 months, 7 days). With STL it may drift slowly across years.
Remainder  RtWhatever T and S don't explain — irregular noise and one-off shocks. A good split leaves R looking like white noise (no pattern left).
the same series read four ways: data = trend-cycle + seasonal + remainder
How the trend gets smoothed
  • classicMoving average (m-MA): average a window to iron out season & noise and reveal the trend. Even periods need a centered 2×m-MA.
  • STLSeasonal-Trend decomposition using Loess: local smoothers instead of a plain average. best default — handles any season length, lets the season change over time, is robust to outliers, and you tune how smooth T and S are.
  • limitsSTL is additive only (take log for multiplicative) and won't handle calendar effects for you.
Adjust & transform before you split or model
  • adjustStrip out known distortions: calendar (trading days), population (use per-capita), inflation (real vs nominal pesos). Clearer signal → better forecast.
  • transformA math transform — log or Box-Cox(λ) — stabilizes the variance so the swings are the same size across the series.
watch — variance-stabilizing transforms fix the spread. Making the mean flat (differencing → stationarity) is a separate tool that comes with ARIMA (ch. 9). Don't merge the two ideas.
Why bother — what you do with the pieces
  • deseasonSeasonally adjusted series = y − S (additive) or y / S (mult.) → see the real movement without the calendar noise.
  • forecastForecast the seasonally-adjusted part (T+R) and the seasonal part separately, then recombine. (§5.7)
✦ Decompose = T (where it heads) + S (what repeats) + R (what's left). Adjust & transform first; a good split leaves R looking like noise.
